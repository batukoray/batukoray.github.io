Current Track

What I'm working on right now.

  1. June 30 – September 1, 2026

    Undergraduate Researcher at Koç University

    Working in the SPADE (Security, Privacy and Data Engineering) Lab at Koç University on membership inference attacks against fine-tuned autoregressive language models. The question is basically: can you tell whether a given piece of text was in a model’s training data just from how it behaves? Turns out they’re worse at keeping secrets than they’d like you to think. Part of the lab's ongoing work on trustworthy language models.

  2. Additional role · July 2026 to Present

    Mathematics Teaching Assistant at Özyeğin University

    Remote, part-time peer-teaching sessions for MATH103 (Single-Variable Calculus), MATH104 (Multivariable Calculus and Infinite Series), MATH212 (Differential Equations), and MATH217 (Probability and Statistics). None of it is a detour from the LLM work. A language model is a probability distribution over tokens, the training objective is a negative log-likelihood, backpropagation is the chain rule over a computational graph, and the membership inference I work on is a statistical test on that distribution. I teach the math the models run on.

  3. LLM Security

    LLM jailbreaking, red-teaming, and malicious use

    Jailbreaking, red-teaming, and the failure modes that show up once you push a system past what it was trained to allow. The point is to map which attack methodologies actually work, and why. That is what tells you how robust a model really is.

  4. Why this lane

    Past the benchmark

    Everything here comes down to one question: what does a model do when you stop being polite to it? Plenty of systems sail through standard benchmarks and still fold under statistical or adversarial scrutiny.

Research Focus

Four questions I keep coming back to.

1

Adversarial attacks on LLMs

Multi-turn jailbreaking strategies that exploit persuasion, context accumulation, and conversational framing rather than purely automated optimization.

2

NLP and transformer architectures

Language modeling, attention, and transformer internals. The architecture side of the same systems I spend the rest of my time attacking.

3

AI safety and alignment evaluation

Red-teaming methodologies, robustness evaluation, and the practical question of how safety fine-tuning can still be circumvented in deployed interfaces.

4

Language models in psychology

A broader pull toward AI in psychology, from mood and affective signals to the harder question of how much clinical and personality structure a model can actually recover from a person's text. The end I find most interesting is whether the frameworks clinicians actually use, the Big Five traits and the DSM personality-disorder clusters, show up in language at all.
